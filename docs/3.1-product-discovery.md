# 3.1 — Product Discovery

## The real problem

Korean learners in Vietnam typically fall into one of two groups with clearly different needs:
- Learners studying for **everyday conversational Korean**.
- Learners preparing for the **TOPIK exam** (level not yet differentiated in v1 — see Open Assumptions).

Both groups share the same friction: existing learning material (textbooks, generic language-learning apps) doesn't let them practice reading with material they already have in hand — a textbook page from class, a handwritten note, a line of dialogue captured from a show. They have to manually retype Korean text if they want to look it up or hear it pronounced, which is slow and discouraging.

## Value proposition

**K-Tutor** helps Korean learners (1) follow a vocabulary/grammar path suited to their personal goal (conversation or TOPIK), and (2) practice reading directly from material they already own, by scanning handwriting or images into text and having AI read it back.

## User personas

| Persona | Goal | Pain point |
|---|---|---|
| Conversation learner | Speak and understand everyday Korean | Off-the-shelf content is generic and disconnected from the material they actually encounter |
| TOPIK learner | Pass the TOPIK exam | Needs an exam-aligned path but has no time to build their own reading practice material |

## Discovery scope (v1)

**In scope:** vocabulary/grammar path for both personas; scanning a handwritten note or image into text (OCR); reading that text aloud via AI (TTS); progress tracking.

**Out of scope (v1), moved to future direction:** reading text back in the user's own voice (voice cloning), automatic pronunciation scoring, a free-form two-way conversational chatbot, advanced TOPIK content (levels 5–6).

## Success signal

A learner selects a goal (conversation / TOPIK) → receives a vocabulary/grammar path → scans a piece of text → hears it read back by AI → can see their own learning progress.

## Open assumptions

- Specific TOPIK level (I or II) is not differentiated in v1 — v1 targets both, without a level-specific curriculum yet.
- Vocabulary/grammar data source: not yet selected; the leading candidate is the open API from South Korea's National Institute of Korean Language, pending a technical evaluation in Chapter 5.
- Platform: **Web first (v1, for course CS2028)**; a Mobile version is a future direction for a different course, not part of v1.
